# RedNote Monthly Report

Produce the written analysis for one account and reporting month. Analyze each post before writing **本月账号状态分析**. This skill does not create or edit slides, Canva designs, or PPT files.

## Verify the source files first

The user must save legible backend screenshots as files, grouped so each screenshot can be mapped to its account, post, and reporting period. A practical layout is one month folder with `Overall Account Data/`, one folder per post, and `PM and Comments/` when those records exist. Exact folder names do not matter. Extract an archive if needed, then inspect its contents. Do not treat a filename as proof of the metric or date inside an image.

Establish the expected post list from a posting calendar, backend publishing view, or the user's list. Compare it with the post folders. If no source establishes the full list, **ask the user to confirm that all posts for the month are present**. Separate posts published during the reporting month from older posts whose cumulative screenshots were updated later. Analyze both when supplied, but label older posts as historical references and exclude them from the month's new-post count and totals.

Check that each post has enough readable evidence to identify its title/topic, publish date, screenshot cutoff, reach and views, cover click-through, engagement counts/rate, follows, traffic sources, and available retention metrics (2-second exit, 5-second completion, average watch, full completion). Check overall account screenshots for their date window, reach and views, click-through, viewing, engagement, follower growth, profile visits/conversion, and traffic sources. Use audience demographics only when available and relevant. **Ask whether there are PM/comment records if no such files are supplied; a missing folder is not proof that there were no inquiries.**

Make a compact internal coverage list: post/account area, screenshot date, fields found, missing or unreadable fields, and conflicting values. For **any missing screenshot or needed metric**, ask the user for the specific backend tab and date range, or ask them to confirm that the data is unavailable. Do not silently proceed with a complete-sounding report while the gap is unresolved. Work on complete posts while waiting; finish the report after the user supplies the evidence or confirms the gap. Never replace an absent value with zero or an estimate.

## Analyze every post

Read the user's latest benchmark; when none is supplied, use [references/benchmark.md](references/benchmark.md). The benchmark is a working comparison framework, not an official platform distribution rule. Use one consistent detailed metrics panel when a diagnostic card and detail card disagree. Record material discrepancies. Keep counts, percentages, percentage-point changes, and date windows distinct. Use platform-displayed rates; do not invent a denominator for follower growth rate or recompute an unclear engagement rate.

For each post, write roughly one slide's worth of client-facing Chinese copy, using relevant headings such as **整体表现**, **流量表现**, and **内容与互动**. Explain what the metrics show about this post's topic, format, and objective. Evaluate retention in the context of the actual video length; do not assume every account uses 30–40-second videos. If the outcome is good for the post's role, end with **表现亮点** or a concise observation and omit **后续优化方向**. For a weaker result, include a specific **后续优化方向** tied to the evidence. Avoid generic calls to “优化封面/标题/互动” without saying what should change and why.

Use a constructive client-facing tone when a result is weak. State the actual number and what it means, then place a practical suggestion alongside the issue. Highlight any genuine strength that the same post shows, without calling a weak metric good or concealing a decline. Prefer “目前互动率为 3.8%，开头留存较稳；结尾可加入明确的选择题，争取更多评论” over “互动表现差，内容没有吸引力”. Avoid blame, alarmist labels, and repeated negative judgments. A strong post needs a clear account of why it worked, not a forced improvement section.

Treat recommendation and search shares as sources of views, not proof of why an algorithm distributed a post. Do not call a post viral merely because its early retention is strong. Compare posts with different snapshot dates only with that limitation; do not sum their cumulative data into a monthly result.

## Write 本月账号状态分析 last

Synthesize the completed post analyses with the overall account figures. Identify content patterns associated with greater reach, retention, saves, follows, and inquiries, while distinguishing an observed pattern from a proven cause. Use PM/comments when present to describe actual questions and lead intent, such as store location, price, online ordering, product specifications, catalog, or wholesale. Do not attribute a conversation to a particular post unless the evidence links them. Inquiries are not confirmed orders. Omit customer names, phone numbers, and private identifiers from client copy unless the user specifically needs them.

Write concise, client-ready Chinese analysis in the established monthly-report style. Use the account's reporting window and post cutoff dates accurately. Do not mention internal files, screenshots, benchmark PDFs, or phrases such as “在 PDF 所列” in the client-facing copy. Keep methodological notes and evidence gaps separate from the copy, except when a limitation changes the client-facing conclusion.

Apply the same constructive tone to the account summary: pair a decline or bottleneck with the content pattern, audience response, or specific action that offers a path forward. Do not turn a downward trend into a positive claim. Keep the wording confident and measured so the client can see both the result and the response to it.

Return the per-post analyses, then **本月账号状态分析**, plus a short list of unresolved source gaps or decisions. Save written files only when the user asks for files or the established project workflow calls for them. Do not create slides under this skill.
